Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide, with types like lung, prostate, and colorectal cancers contributing to a significant portion of cancer-related mortality. Globally, cancer statistics highlight the alarming rate at which these diseases are claiming lives. Cancer is responsible for a substantial number of deaths across all demographics, emphasizing the importance of early detection to improve survival rates. Previous global studies have demonstrated the escalating incidence of cancer, further underlining the need for innovative methods to predict and detect cancer in its early stages.

Traditional statistical approaches used to assess cancer risks often struggle with the high-dimensionality and complexity of medical data, which limits their effectiveness. Machine learning techniques offer an opportunity to overcome these challenges by providing more accurate predictions through advanced algorithms that can manage and analyze complex datasets. These methods have been successfully applied to cancer diagnosis and survival prediction, showing potential for significant improvements in early-stage detection. By integrating machine learning models into healthcare systems, it is possible to identify patients at higher risk of developing cancer early, thus improving treatment outcomes and potentially saving lives.

Therefore, the focus of this project is to develop a machine learning-based cancer prediction model that can effectively handle high-dimensional medical data and accurately predict cancer risks at early stages.
